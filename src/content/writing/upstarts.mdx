You have no users, no data, and no distribution. Conventionally that's three
problems. In the Atmosphere it's mostly one, and it's smaller than it looks.

The default startup path is to rebuild the entire stack — accounts, storage,
social graph, feed, moderation — before you get to the idea you actually had.
That work isn't differentiated. It's a tax, it takes a year, and at the end of
it you still have zero users.

## What you don't have to build

Identity exists. Storage exists. Aggregation exists. A network of people with
portable accounts already exists, and they can use your app without creating
anything.

That last part is the one worth sitting with. Your cold start isn't cold. The
users are already there, holding identities and data they can point at whatever
you ship. You are not asking them to sign up. You are asking them to try
something.

That changes what your first version has to be. Not a platform. A feature that
happens to be worth opening.

## Pick your layer

The discipline is choosing exactly one layer to be excellent at, and taking the
other three off the shelf.

Most upstarts should pick the app layer, because that's where the idea usually
lives and where the feedback loop is tightest. Some should pick data — if your
insight is genuinely a new record type, defining that Lexicon well is the
company. Very few should pick relay, and if you think you should, the honest
version of that argument is usually about cost, not product.

If you can't say which layer you're competing on in one sentence, you're
building four mediocre things.

## The Lexicon decision

You'll make one schema decision in week one that you'll live with for years.

Use an existing Lexicon where one fits, even imperfectly — you inherit every app
that already reads it, and that inheritance is worth more than the fit. Define
your own only where nothing exists, and then define it as though someone else
will implement it, because if the idea is any good, someone will.

The upstart advantage is that this is cheap for you and expensive for everyone
else. You have no legacy schema to reconcile. Spend that.

## Layer by layer

### Identity

Don't build auth. Take DIDs, take domain handles, ship on day one.

> **Story slot — identity.** *(TK — workshop.)*

### Data storage

Records go in the user's repository, not your database. Your infrastructure bill
scales with what you compute, not with what people keep.

> **Story slot — data.** *(TK — workshop.)*

### Relay

Consume the firehose, don't run one. You want the whole network's activity
without the whole network's operating cost.

> team that built a real product on a public feed before writing any backend.)*

### App layer

Everything you have goes here.

## First moves

Ship something in a fortnight that a stranger on the network can use without
signing up. Then look at what they did with it. That's a faster, truer signal
than anything you'd have learned in the year you didn't spend building
accounts.
